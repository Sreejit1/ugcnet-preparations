Unit I – Teaching Aptitude

1. Teaching

1.1 Concept of Teaching

Teaching is a systematic and purposeful process in which a teacher facilitates learning by providing appropriate knowledge, experiences, guidance, activities and learning resources.

Teaching is not simply the transfer of information from a teacher to a learner. It involves interaction between the teacher, learner, learning materials and learning environment.

Important Characteristics of Teaching

  1. Teaching is a purposeful activity.
  2. Teaching aims to bring about desirable changes in learners.
  3. Teaching involves interaction between the teacher and learner.
  4. Teaching is both an art and a science.
  5. Teaching involves planning and organization.
  6. Teaching requires communication.
  7. Teaching is influenced by the learner’s characteristics.
  8. Teaching involves evaluation of learning.
  9. Teaching may occur through face-to-face or online modes.
  10. Teaching is a continuous process.

2. Objectives of Teaching

The objectives of teaching describe what learners are expected to achieve after instruction.

Major objectives include:

2.1 Knowledge

Teaching helps learners acquire:

  • Facts
  • Concepts
  • Principles
  • Theories
  • Information

2.2 Understanding

Learners should be able to:

  • Explain concepts
  • Interpret information
  • Compare ideas
  • Classify information
  • Summarize concepts

2.3 Application

Learners should be able to apply knowledge to:

  • Solve problems
  • Perform tasks
  • Handle real-life situations
  • Use theories in practical situations

2.4 Skill Development

Teaching develops:

  • Technical skills
  • Communication skills
  • Problem-solving skills
  • Analytical skills
  • Practical skills

2.5 Critical Thinking

Teaching should encourage learners to:

  • Analyze information
  • Question assumptions
  • Evaluate evidence
  • Develop arguments
  • Make informed decisions

2.6 Attitude and Values

Education also contributes to the development of:

  • Ethical values
  • Social responsibility
  • Cooperation
  • Respect
  • Professional attitudes

3. Levels of Teaching

There are three important levels of teaching:

  1. Memory Level
  2. Understanding Level
  3. Reflective Level

These levels represent increasing levels of intellectual activity.

3.1 Memory Level of Teaching

Memory-level teaching emphasizes the recall and reproduction of previously learned information.

The learner mainly remembers facts, definitions, formulas, rules and information.

Characteristics

  • Focuses on memorization.
  • Teacher generally plays a dominant role.
  • Learner activity is comparatively limited.
  • Emphasis is placed on recall.
  • Useful for basic factual knowledge.

Examples

A teacher asks:

What is the definition of Artificial Intelligence?

The student recalls the definition and provides the answer.

Another example:

What is the formula for the mean?

The student recalls the formula.

Advantages

  • Useful for acquiring basic facts.
  • Provides foundational knowledge.
  • Useful when learners need to remember terminology and definitions.

Limitations

  • May encourage rote learning.
  • Does not necessarily develop critical thinking.
  • Limited opportunity for independent problem solving.

Key Point

Memory level = Recall and reproduction of information.

4. Understanding Level of Teaching

Understanding-level teaching goes beyond simple memorization.

The learner is expected to understand the meaning of information and explain it in their own words.

Characteristics

  • Focuses on comprehension.
  • Learners understand relationships between concepts.
  • Encourages explanation and interpretation.
  • Greater interaction may occur between teacher and learner.
  • Knowledge can be applied to familiar situations.

Examples

Instead of asking:

What is machine learning?

the teacher may ask:

Explain how machine learning differs from traditional programming.

The student must understand the concept and explain the difference.

Activities

Understanding-level teaching may involve:

  • Explanation
  • Comparison
  • Classification
  • Interpretation
  • Illustration
  • Summarization

Key Point

Understanding level = Meaning, explanation and interpretation.

5. Reflective Level of Teaching

Reflective-level teaching is the highest level among the three levels of teaching.

It focuses on:

  • Critical thinking
  • Problem solving
  • Analysis
  • Evaluation
  • Independent thinking

The learner does not simply recall or explain information. The learner examines a problem, considers alternatives and develops a reasoned conclusion.

Example

Suppose the topic is Artificial Intelligence.

Instead of asking:

What is Artificial Intelligence?

or:

Explain Artificial Intelligence.

a reflective question would be:

What ethical problems may arise when Artificial Intelligence is used for decision-making?

The learner needs to:

  1. Understand the problem.
  2. Identify relevant information.
  3. Analyze different viewpoints.
  4. Evaluate possible consequences.
  5. Develop a reasoned conclusion.

Characteristics

  • Learner-centred.
  • Problem-oriented.
  • Encourages critical thinking.
  • Encourages independent thinking.
  • Promotes creativity.
  • Requires higher-order thinking.
  • Teacher acts more as a facilitator.

Key Point

Reflective level = Critical thinking, problem solving and independent thinking.

7. Characteristics and Basic Requirements of Teaching

Effective teaching requires several basic elements.

7.1 Teacher

The teacher should have:

  • Subject knowledge
  • Communication skills
  • Teaching skills
  • Classroom management skills
  • Motivation
  • Professional ethics

7.2 Learner

Teaching must consider:

  • Ability
  • Previous knowledge
  • Motivation
  • Interest
  • Learning needs
  • Individual differences

7.3 Objectives

Teaching should have clearly defined learning objectives.

For example:

After completing the lesson, students should be able to explain the difference between supervised and unsupervised learning.

7.4 Learning Materials

Examples include:

  • Textbooks
  • Notes
  • Presentations
  • Videos
  • Charts
  • Digital resources

7.5 Learning Environment

A good learning environment should encourage:

  • Participation
  • Questions
  • Discussion
  • Collaboration
  • Respect

7.6 Evaluation

Teaching should include appropriate evaluation to determine whether learning objectives have been achieved.

8. Learner’s Characteristics

The syllabus specifically includes characteristics of:

  • Adolescent learners
  • Adult learners
  • Individual differences

Learners are not identical. They differ in their abilities, experiences, interests, motivation and learning needs.

9. Characteristics of Adolescent Learners

Adolescence is a developmental stage associated with significant physical, emotional, social and cognitive changes.

9.1 Academic Characteristics

Adolescent learners may:

  • Develop greater academic abilities.
  • Begin to develop specialized interests.
  • Develop independent study habits.
  • Differ considerably in academic performance.

9.2 Social Characteristics

Adolescents may:

  • Give importance to peer relationships.
  • Seek acceptance from others.
  • Develop social identities.
  • Participate actively in group activities.

9.3 Emotional Characteristics

Adolescents may experience:

  • Emotional changes
  • Increased sensitivity
  • Desire for independence
  • Self-consciousness
  • Changes in confidence

9.4 Cognitive Characteristics

Cognitive development includes increasing ability to:

  • Think logically
  • Analyze information
  • Understand abstract concepts
  • Consider different viewpoints
  • Solve increasingly complex problems

10. Characteristics of Adult Learners

Adult learners generally bring previous knowledge, experience and expectations into the learning environment.

Important Characteristics

Adult learners are often:

  • Experience-oriented
  • Goal-oriented
  • Practical
  • More self-directed
  • Interested in relevant learning
  • Motivated by personal or professional needs

10.1 Academic Characteristics

Adult learners may:

  • Connect new knowledge with previous experience.
  • Prefer practical applications.
  • Use existing knowledge while learning new concepts.

10.2 Social Characteristics

Adult learners may have:

  • Professional responsibilities
  • Family responsibilities
  • Social responsibilities
  • Workplace experiences

10.3 Emotional Characteristics

Adult learners may value:

  • Respect
  • Independence
  • Recognition of their experience
  • Meaningful participation

10.4 Cognitive Characteristics

Adult learners can use previous experience to:

  • Analyze problems
  • Compare alternatives
  • Evaluate information
  • Apply knowledge to practical situations

11. Individual Differences

Individual differences refer to the differences that exist among learners.

Learners may differ in:

  • Intelligence
  • Aptitude
  • Personality
  • Motivation
  • Interest
  • Previous knowledge
  • Learning ability
  • Learning pace
  • Language ability
  • Socio-economic background
  • Experience

Example

Consider two students learning Python.

Student A already knows C programming.

Student B has never studied programming.

Giving exactly the same explanation at exactly the same pace may not be equally effective for both students.

Therefore, effective teaching should recognize individual differences.

Implication for Teachers

Teachers can address individual differences through:

  • Different teaching methods
  • Remedial instruction
  • Additional learning resources
  • Group activities
  • Individual guidance
  • Differentiated learning activities

12. Factors Affecting Teaching

The syllabus identifies six major areas:

  1. Teacher
  2. Learner
  3. Support material
  4. Instructional facilities
  5. Learning environment
  6. Institution

12.1 Teacher-Related Factors

Teaching effectiveness may be influenced by:

  • Subject knowledge
  • Teaching experience
  • Communication ability
  • Motivation
  • Personality
  • Classroom management
  • Teaching methodology
  • Attitude towards learners

A teacher with strong subject knowledge but poor communication may still face difficulties in teaching effectively.

13. Learner-Related Factors

Important learner-related factors include:

  • Motivation
  • Interest
  • Intelligence
  • Previous knowledge
  • Learning ability
  • Attention
  • Physical and emotional condition
  • Attendance
  • Participation

A motivated learner generally participates more actively in learning activities.

14. Support Material

Support materials help teachers communicate concepts effectively.

Examples:

  • Textbooks
  • Reference books
  • Lecture notes
  • PowerPoint presentations
  • Videos
  • Charts
  • Models
  • Online resources
  • Digital libraries

Good Support Material

Good instructional material should be:

  • Relevant
  • Accurate
  • Clear
  • Well organized
  • Accessible
  • Appropriate to the learner’s level

15. Instructional Facilities

Instructional facilities include the physical and technological resources available for teaching.

Examples:

  • Classrooms
  • Laboratories
  • Computers
  • Projectors
  • Smart boards
  • Internet
  • Libraries
  • Learning Management Systems

Adequate facilities can support effective teaching and learning.

16. Learning Environment

The learning environment includes the physical, social and psychological conditions in which learning takes place.

Characteristics of a Positive Learning Environment

  • Safe
  • Inclusive
  • Supportive
  • Interactive
  • Respectful
  • Encourages questions
  • Encourages participation

A negative learning environment can reduce learner participation and motivation.

17. Institutional Factors

Institutional factors include:

  • Institutional policies
  • Administration
  • Infrastructure
  • Academic culture
  • Teacher workload
  • Class size
  • Curriculum
  • Examination system
  • Availability of resources

These factors can influence the effectiveness of teaching.

18. Methods of Teaching in Institutions of Higher Learning

The syllabus includes two important comparisons:

  1. Teacher-centred vs learner-centred methods
  2. Offline vs online methods

19. Teacher-Centred Methods

In teacher-centred teaching, the teacher plays the primary role in organizing and delivering instruction.

Examples

  • Lecture
  • Direct instruction
  • Demonstration

Characteristics

  • Teacher controls the lesson.
  • Information generally flows from teacher to learner.
  • Useful for delivering information to large groups.
  • Learner participation may be relatively limited.

Advantages

  • Efficient for large classes.
  • Allows systematic presentation of content.
  • Useful for introducing new topics.

Limitations

  • May encourage passive learning.
  • Limited individual interaction.
  • May not sufficiently develop problem-solving skills.

20. Learner-Centred Methods

Learner-centred teaching places greater emphasis on the active participation of learners.

Examples

  • Discussion
  • Project-based learning
  • Problem-based learning
  • Case study
  • Collaborative learning
  • Seminar
  • Group work

Characteristics

  • Learner actively participates.
  • Teacher acts as facilitator.
  • Encourages discussion.
  • Encourages problem solving.
  • Promotes independent learning.

Advantages

  • Encourages active learning.
  • Develops communication skills.
  • Develops collaboration.
  • Encourages critical thinking.
  • Promotes learner autonomy.

Limitations

  • May require more time.
  • Requires careful planning.
  • Can be difficult with very large groups.
  • Requires active participation from learners.

21. Teacher-Centred vs Learner-Centred

Teacher-Centred Learner-Centred
Teacher is central Learner is central
Teacher mainly delivers content Learner actively participates
More lecture-oriented More activity-oriented
Teacher controls learning Learner has greater responsibility
Useful for large-scale content delivery Useful for active and collaborative learning

22. Offline Teaching

Offline teaching generally refers to face-to-face teaching in a physical learning environment.

Examples:

  • Classroom teaching
  • Laboratory teaching
  • Seminars
  • Workshops
  • Tutorials

Advantages

  • Direct interaction
  • Immediate feedback
  • Face-to-face communication
  • Easier classroom management
  • Social interaction

Limitations

  • Requires physical presence.
  • Limited by location and timetable.
  • May require significant infrastructure.

23. Online Teaching

Online teaching uses digital technologies and networks to facilitate learning.

Examples include:

  • Online courses
  • Video lectures
  • Virtual classrooms
  • Learning Management Systems
  • MOOCs
  • Online assessments

24. SWAYAM

SWAYAM stands for:

Study Webs of Active Learning for Young Aspiring Minds.

It is an online learning initiative that provides access to educational courses.

25. SWAYAM Prabha

SWAYAM Prabha is a digital educational initiative that provides educational content through television channels.

It supports access to educational resources through digital broadcasting.

26. MOOCs

MOOC stands for:

Massive Open Online Course.

MOOCs are online courses designed to provide learning opportunities to a large number of learners.

Characteristics

  • Online
  • Large-scale participation
  • Accessible through the internet
  • Course-based
  • Often includes videos, readings, quizzes and assessments

29. Evaluation Systems

Evaluation is the systematic process of determining the extent to which learning objectives have been achieved.

Evaluation helps teachers determine:

  • What learners have learned.
  • Whether objectives have been achieved.
  • Where learners have difficulties.
  • Whether teaching methods are effective.

30. Elements of Evaluation

Important elements include:

Learning Objectives

What should learners achieve?

Assessment

How will learning be measured?

Measurement

What numerical or descriptive information is obtained?

Interpretation

What does the assessment result mean?

Decision Making

What action should be taken based on the results?

31. Types of Evaluation

Formative Evaluation

Formative evaluation takes place during the teaching-learning process.

Examples:

  • Class tests
  • Quizzes
  • Assignments
  • Classroom activities
  • Feedback

Purpose

To identify learning difficulties and improve learning while instruction is continuing.

Summative Evaluation

Summative evaluation generally occurs at the end of a course or instructional period.

Examples:

  • Semester examination
  • Final examination
  • End-of-course assessment

Purpose

To determine the level of achievement after instruction.

Diagnostic Evaluation

Diagnostic evaluation is used to identify specific learning difficulties.

Example:

A teacher gives a test to determine why students are having difficulty understanding probability.

32. Evaluation in Choice Based Credit System

The Choice Based Credit System (CBCS) provides flexibility in higher education through a credit-based structure.

Evaluation may include components such as:

  • Internal assessment
  • Assignments
  • Tests
  • Projects
  • Presentations
  • Semester-end examinations

The exact implementation can vary according to institutional and regulatory requirements.

33. Computer-Based Testing

Computer-based testing (CBT) uses computers to conduct examinations and assessments.

Characteristics

  • Questions are displayed digitally.
  • Candidates enter answers using a computer.
  • Responses can be stored electronically.
  • Results may be processed digitally.

Advantages

  • Reduces paper usage.
  • Efficient administration.
  • Faster processing.
  • Can support different question formats.
  • Digital storage of responses.

Limitations

  • Requires appropriate infrastructure.
  • Requires computer literacy.
  • Technical problems may affect examination.
  • Requires reliable power and network infrastructure where applicable.

34. Innovations in Evaluation Systems

Modern evaluation systems may include:

  • Online assessment
  • Computer-based testing
  • Continuous assessment
  • Digital portfolios
  • Online quizzes
  • Automated assessment
  • Project-based assessment
  • Peer assessment
  • Formative feedback

Technology can make assessment more flexible and can provide faster feedback.
