Unit IX – People, Development and Environment

1. Introduction

People, development and environment are closely connected.

Development improves human life through:

  • Better education
  • Better healthcare
  • Employment
  • Transportation
  • Industries
  • Technology
  • Housing
  • Communication

However, development can also put pressure on the environment.

For example:

More industries → more production → more employment → but possibly more air and water pollution.

Therefore, modern development aims to achieve development without destroying the ability of future generations to meet their needs.

This idea is called Sustainable Development.

2. Development and Environment

2.1 What is Development?

Development means improvement in the economic, social and living conditions of people.

Development can include:

  • Economic growth
  • Better education
  • Better healthcare
  • Better infrastructure
  • Reduction of poverty
  • Employment opportunities
  • Improved quality of life

Example

Construction of a new hospital can contribute to development because it provides:

  • Healthcare
  • Employment
  • Better medical facilities

But construction may also affect:

  • Land
  • Trees
  • Water
  • Local ecosystems

Therefore, development and environmental protection must be considered together.

3. Millennium Development Goals (MDGs)

The Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) were a set of international development goals established under the United Nations framework.

They focused mainly on improving human development and reducing major global problems.

There were 8 Millennium Development Goals.

The 8 MDGs

No. Millennium Development Goal
1 Eradicate extreme poverty and hunger
2 Achieve universal primary education
3 Promote gender equality and empower women
4 Reduce child mortality
5 Improve maternal health
6 Combat HIV/AIDS, malaria and other diseases
7 Ensure environmental sustainability
8 Develop a global partnership for development

UGC NET Point

Remember:

MDGs = 8 goals

4. Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)

The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) are global goals adopted by the United Nations to promote sustainable development.

There are 17 SDGs.

They address:

  • Poverty
  • Hunger
  • Health
  • Education
  • Gender equality
  • Water
  • Energy
  • Employment
  • Infrastructure
  • Inequality
  • Cities
  • Consumption
  • Climate
  • Oceans
  • Forests
  • Peace
  • Partnerships

17 Sustainable Development Goals

No. SDG
1 No Poverty
2 Zero Hunger
3 Good Health and Well-being
4 Quality Education
5 Gender Equality
6 Clean Water and Sanitation
7 Affordable and Clean Energy
8 Decent Work and Economic Growth
9 Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure
10 Reduced Inequalities
11 Sustainable Cities and Communities

13 Climate Action

15 Life on Land

Important Difference

MDGs SDGs
8 goals 17 goals
Earlier global development framework Current global development framework
Focused strongly on major development problems Broader economic, social and environmental focus
Preceded SDGs Followed MDGs

MDG → 8

SDG → 17

5. Sustainable Development

5.1 Meaning

Sustainable development means development that meets present needs while protecting the ability of future generations to meet their needs.

In simple words:

Development today should not destroy tomorrow’s resources.

Three major dimensions

Sustainable development generally involves three interconnected dimensions:

  1. Economic
  2. Social
  3. Environmental

This is sometimes represented as:

People + Planet + Prosperity

Example

A solar power project can:

  • Produce energy
  • Create employment
  • Reduce dependence on fossil fuels

Therefore, it can support sustainable development when properly planned.

6. Human and Environment Interaction

Humans depend on the environment for:

  • Food
  • Water
  • Air
  • Energy
  • Minerals
  • Forest products
  • Land
  • Biodiversity

At the same time, human activities modify the environment.

This creates a continuous interaction between humans and nature.

Simple cycle

Human needs → Human activities → Environmental changes → Effects on humans

7. Anthropogenic Activities

The word anthropogenic means caused by human activities.

Anthropogenic activities include:

  • Industrialization
  • Urbanization
  • Deforestation
  • Mining
  • Agriculture
  • Transportation
  • Construction
  • Burning of fossil fuels
  • Waste generation
  • Overuse of natural resources

8. Impacts of Anthropogenic Activities

8.1 Deforestation

Deforestation means removal of forests.

Causes

  • Agriculture
  • Urbanization
  • Roads
  • Industries
  • Mining
  • Construction
  • Timber extraction

Effects

  • Loss of biodiversity
  • Soil erosion
  • Habitat destruction
  • Changes in water cycle
  • Increased carbon dioxide
  • Climate impacts

9. Industrialization

Industrialization can improve:

  • Employment
  • Production
  • Infrastructure
  • Economic growth

But uncontrolled industrialization may cause:

  • Air pollution
  • Water pollution
  • Soil pollution
  • Noise pollution
  • Hazardous waste
  • Greenhouse gas emissions

10. Urbanization

Urbanization means growth of towns and cities.

Benefits

  • Better infrastructure
  • Employment
  • Education
  • Healthcare
  • Transportation

Environmental problems

  • Traffic pollution
  • Waste generation
  • Water demand
  • Noise
  • Loss of agricultural land
  • Urban heat
  • Pressure on natural resources

11. Environmental Issues

Environmental issues can be classified according to geographical scale.

11.1 Local Environmental Issues

Problems affecting a small geographical area.

Examples:

  • Local water pollution
  • Noise pollution near highways
  • Garbage accumulation
  • Local industrial pollution

11.2 Regional Environmental Issues

Problems affecting a larger region.

Examples:

  • River pollution across several districts
  • Regional air pollution
  • Deforestation across a state or region
  • Water scarcity across a region

11.3 Global Environmental Issues

Problems affecting the entire planet or many countries.

Examples:

  • Climate change
  • Global warming
  • Ozone layer depletion
  • Loss of biodiversity
  • Ocean pollution

Exam Tip

Local → Small area

Regional → Larger geographical region

Global → Planet-wide

12. Air Pollution

Air pollution occurs when harmful substances enter the atmosphere in concentrations that can cause harmful effects.

Major air pollutants

  • Particulate matter
  • Carbon monoxide (CO)
  • Sulphur dioxide (SO₂)
  • Nitrogen oxides (NOₓ)
  • Ozone
  • Lead
  • Other harmful gases and particles

Sources

Natural sources

  • Forest fires
  • Volcanic activity
  • Dust storms

Human sources

  • Vehicles
  • Industries
  • Thermal power plants
  • Construction
  • Burning of fuels
  • Burning of waste

13. Effects of Air Pollution

Air pollution can affect:

Human health

  • Breathing problems
  • Asthma
  • Respiratory diseases
  • Cardiovascular problems
  • Eye irritation

Environment

  • Acid deposition
  • Reduced visibility
  • Damage to vegetation
  • Climate effects

14. Water Pollution

Water pollution occurs when harmful substances contaminate water bodies.

Sources include:

  • Industrial wastewater
  • Domestic sewage
  • Agricultural runoff
  • Oil spills
  • Plastics
  • Mining activities

Effects

  • Unsafe drinking water
  • Waterborne diseases
  • Death of aquatic organisms
  • Eutrophication
  • Reduction in water quality

15. Eutrophication

Eutrophication is the excessive enrichment of a water body with nutrients, especially nitrogen and phosphorus.

Process

Excess nutrients

↓

Rapid growth of algae

↓

Algal bloom

↓

Algae die

↓

Microorganisms decompose them

↓

Oxygen decreases

↓

Aquatic organisms may die

Important term

Eutrophication → Nutrient enrichment → Algal growth → Oxygen depletion

16. Soil Pollution

Soil pollution occurs when harmful substances contaminate soil.

Causes

  • Excessive pesticides
  • Chemical fertilizers
  • Industrial waste
  • Mining
  • Plastic waste
  • Improper disposal of hazardous materials

Effects

  • Reduced soil fertility
  • Contamination of crops
  • Groundwater contamination
  • Harm to soil organisms
  • Human health effects

17. Noise Pollution

Noise pollution means unwanted or excessive sound that can negatively affect humans and other organisms.

Sources

  • Traffic
  • Aircraft
  • Industries
  • Construction
  • Loudspeakers
  • Machines

Effects

  • Hearing problems
  • Sleep disturbance
  • Stress
  • Reduced concentration
  • Communication problems

18. Waste

Waste is unwanted material generated from human activities.

Important categories include:

  1. Solid waste
  2. Liquid waste
  3. Biomedical waste
  4. Hazardous waste
  5. Electronic waste

19. Solid Waste

Solid waste includes discarded solid materials.

Examples:

  • Food waste
  • Paper
  • Plastic
  • Glass
  • Metal
  • Packaging materials

Management

Important methods include:

  • Reduce
  • Reuse
  • Recycle
  • Composting
  • Proper collection
  • Scientific disposal

Important concept

3Rs = Reduce + Reuse + Recycle

20. Liquid Waste

Liquid waste includes wastewater generated from:

  • Houses
  • Industries
  • Hospitals
  • Agriculture
  • Commercial establishments

Improper disposal can contaminate:

  • Rivers
  • Lakes
  • Groundwater
  • Soil

Treatment of wastewater is therefore important.

21. Biomedical Waste

Biomedical waste is waste generated during:

  • Diagnosis
  • Treatment
  • Immunization
  • Medical research
  • Healthcare activities

Examples:

  • Used gloves
  • Syringes
  • Bandages
  • Contaminated materials
  • Certain laboratory waste

Improper handling can cause:

  • Infection
  • Injury
  • Environmental contamination

22. Hazardous Waste

Hazardous waste is waste that can pose significant risks to human health or the environment because of its properties.

It may be:

  • Toxic
  • Corrosive
  • Flammable
  • Reactive

Examples:

  • Certain industrial chemicals
  • Solvents
  • Some chemical wastes
  • Certain laboratory wastes

Hazardous waste requires specialized handling and disposal.

23. Electronic Waste (E-Waste)

E-waste means discarded electrical and electronic equipment.

Examples:

  • Computers
  • Mobile phones
  • Printers
  • Televisions
  • Batteries
  • Circuit boards

Problems

Electronic waste can contain hazardous substances.

Improper disposal can cause:

  • Soil contamination
  • Water contamination
  • Air pollution
  • Human health risks

E-waste management

  • Repair
  • Reuse
  • Recycling
  • Proper collection
  • Authorized disposal

24. Climate Change

Climate change refers to long-term changes in climate patterns.

Human activities, particularly the emission of greenhouse gases, have contributed significantly to recent climate change.

Major greenhouse gases

  • Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
  • Methane (CH₄)
  • Nitrous oxide (N₂O)
  • Certain industrial gases

25. Greenhouse Effect

The greenhouse effect is the warming effect produced when certain gases in the atmosphere absorb and re-emit infrared radiation.

Natural greenhouse effect

The natural greenhouse effect makes Earth suitable for life.

Enhanced greenhouse effect

Human activities increase the concentration of greenhouse gases, strengthening the warming effect.

26. Global Warming

Global warming refers specifically to the long-term increase in Earth’s average surface temperature.

Difference

Climate change is broader.

It includes changes in:

  • Temperature
  • Rainfall
  • Weather patterns
  • Sea level
  • Ice
  • Ecosystems

Global warming specifically refers to long-term warming.

27. Socio-Economic Dimensions of Climate Change

Climate change can affect:

Agriculture

  • Changes in rainfall
  • Heat stress
  • Crop productivity
  • Water availability

Health

  • Heat-related illness
  • Spread of some vector-borne diseases
  • Food and water-related health risks

Economy

  • Damage to infrastructure
  • Agricultural losses
  • Disaster recovery costs
  • Reduced productivity

Employment

Some sectors may face changes in:

  • Productivity
  • Working conditions
  • Employment opportunities

28. Political Dimensions of Climate Change

Climate change has political dimensions because countries must negotiate:

  • Emission reductions
  • Climate finance
  • Technology transfer
  • Adaptation
  • Loss and damage
  • Responsibilities between developed and developing countries

A major issue is the question of:

Who should contribute how much to solving the climate problem?

Countries may have different historical emissions, economic capacities and development needs.

29. Impacts of Pollutants on Human Health

Pollutants can enter the human body through:

  • Breathing
  • Drinking
  • Eating contaminated food
  • Skin contact

Air pollutants

May affect:

  • Lungs
  • Heart
  • Brain
  • Eyes

Water pollutants

May cause:

  • Gastrointestinal diseases
  • Infections
  • Chemical toxicity

Soil pollutants

May enter humans through:

Soil → Crops → Food → Human body

Noise pollution

May cause:

  • Hearing impairment
  • Stress
  • Sleep disturbance

30. Natural Resources

Natural resources are materials and systems obtained from nature and used by humans.

Examples:

  • Water
  • Soil
  • Forests
  • Minerals
  • Sunlight
  • Wind
  • Biomass

They can broadly be classified as:

Renewable resources

Resources that can naturally replenish over time.

Examples:

  • Solar
  • Wind
  • Hydro
  • Biomass
  • Forests, when sustainably managed

Non-renewable resources

Resources that are limited and form very slowly.

Examples:

  • Coal
  • Petroleum
  • Natural gas
  • Many mineral resources

31. Solar Energy

Solar energy comes from the Sun.

It can be converted into electricity using solar photovoltaic cells.

Advantages

  • Renewable
  • Abundant
  • Low operational emissions
  • Useful in remote areas

Limitations

  • Depends on sunlight
  • Requires suitable land/space
  • Energy storage may be required

32. Wind Energy

Wind energy uses moving air to generate electricity.

Wind turbines convert wind energy into electrical energy.

Advantages

  • Renewable
  • Low operational emissions
  • No fuel combustion

Limitations

  • Depends on wind availability
  • Site-specific
  • May create noise and visual impacts

33. Soil as a Natural Resource

Soil supports:

  • Agriculture
  • Plant growth
  • Water storage
  • Nutrient cycling
  • Ecosystems

Threats to soil

  • Erosion
  • Pollution
  • Salinity
  • Desertification
  • Excessive chemical use

Soil conservation

  • Afforestation
  • Contour farming
  • Terracing
  • Crop rotation
  • Controlled grazing

34. Hydropower

Hydropower generates electricity using moving or falling water.

Advantages

  • Renewable
  • Can generate large amounts of electricity
  • Low direct operational greenhouse gas emissions

Limitations

  • Large projects may displace communities
  • Ecosystem impacts
  • Changes to river systems
  • Dependence on water availability

35. Geothermal Energy

Geothermal energy comes from Earth’s internal heat.

It can be used for:

  • Electricity generation
  • Heating
  • Other thermal applications

Advantages

  • Renewable
  • Reliable in suitable locations
  • Small land footprint for some applications

Limitation

Availability is geographically limited.

36. Biomass Energy

Biomass energy is obtained from biological materials.

Examples:

  • Wood
  • Agricultural residues
  • Animal waste
  • Organic waste

Biomass can be converted into:

  • Heat
  • Electricity
  • Biogas
  • Biofuels

Important

Biomass can be renewable when the resource is managed sustainably.

37. Nuclear Energy

Nuclear energy is produced from nuclear reactions.

Nuclear power plants can generate large amounts of electricity.

Advantages

  • High energy density
  • Low operational greenhouse gas emissions
  • Reliable electricity generation

Concerns

  • Radioactive waste
  • Accident risks
  • High capital cost
  • Long-term waste management

38. Forest Resources

Forests provide:

  • Timber
  • Food
  • Medicines
  • Fuel
  • Biodiversity
  • Carbon storage
  • Soil protection
  • Water regulation

Importance

Forests are important for both human welfare and ecological balance.

Threats

  • Deforestation
  • Illegal logging
  • Forest fires
  • Mining
  • Urban expansion

39. Natural Hazards

A natural hazard is a natural event or process that has the potential to cause harm.

Examples:

  • Earthquake
  • Flood
  • Cyclone
  • Drought
  • Landslide
  • Tsunami
  • Volcanic eruption

Important distinction

Hazard ≠ Disaster

A hazard becomes a disaster when it causes serious disruption, losses and impacts that exceed the affected community’s ability to cope.

40. Disaster Management

Disaster management involves actions taken to:

  • Reduce disaster risk
  • Prepare for disasters
  • Respond during disasters
  • Recover after disasters

Four important stages are:

Mitigation → Preparedness → Response → Recovery

41. Mitigation Strategies

Mitigation means reducing the risk or severity of disasters.

Earthquake

  • Earthquake-resistant buildings
  • Building codes
  • Public awareness
  • Emergency planning

Flood

  • Flood forecasting
  • Drainage improvement
  • Flood-resistant infrastructure
  • Wetland protection
  • Early warning systems

Cyclone

  • Early warning
  • Evacuation
  • Cyclone shelters
  • Strong infrastructure
  • Coastal planning

Drought

  • Water conservation
  • Rainwater harvesting
  • Drought-resistant crops
  • Efficient irrigation

Landslide

  • Slope stabilization
  • Vegetation protection
  • Drainage management
  • Avoiding unsafe construction

Tsunami

  • Early warning systems
  • Coastal evacuation plans
  • Public awareness
  • Safe evacuation routes

42. Environmental Protection Act, 1986

The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 is an important Indian environmental law.

Its broad purpose is to provide protection and improvement of the environment and to address environmental pollution.

Important points

It provides a framework for:

  • Environmental protection
  • Pollution control
  • Regulation of hazardous substances
  • Government action against environmental threats
  • Environmental standards and rules

UGC NET Point

Environment (Protection) Act → 1986

43. National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC)

India’s National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC) provides a framework for addressing climate change.

It includes eight national missions.

Eight National Missions

  1. National Solar Mission
  2. National Mission for Enhanced Energy Efficiency
  3. National Mission on Sustainable Habitat
  4. National Water Mission
  5. National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem
  6. National Mission for a Green India
  7. National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture
  8. National Mission on Strategic Knowledge for Climate Change

Easy memory idea

Solar – Energy – Habitat – Water – Himalaya – Green India – Agriculture – Knowledge

44. Montreal Protocol

The Montreal Protocol is an international agreement designed to protect the ozone layer by controlling and phasing out ozone-depleting substances.

Important point

Montreal Protocol → Ozone layer protection

It is closely associated with substances such as:

  • CFCs
  • Halons
  • Other ozone-depleting substances

Exam trap

Do not confuse:

Montreal Protocol → Ozone depletion

with

Kyoto Protocol / Paris Agreement → Climate change

45. Rio Summit

The United Nations Conference on Environment and Development (UNCED) was held in Rio de Janeiro in 1992.

It is commonly called the Rio Earth Summit.

Major outcomes included:

  • Agenda 21
  • Rio Declaration
  • Convention on Biological Diversity
  • UN Framework Convention on Climate Change

UGC NET Point

Rio Summit → 1992

46. Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)

The Convention on Biological Diversity is an international agreement concerned with biodiversity.

Its major objectives include:

  1. Conservation of biological diversity
  2. Sustainable use of components of biodiversity
  3. Fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from genetic resources

Easy memory

CBD → Biodiversity

47. Kyoto Protocol

The Kyoto Protocol is an international agreement under the UN climate framework.

It established legally binding emission reduction commitments for developed countries/Parties listed under the protocol’s framework.

Important association

Kyoto Protocol → Greenhouse gas emissions / Climate change

Exam Tip

Remember:

Montreal → Ozone

Kyoto → Climate

48. Paris Agreement

The Paris Agreement is an international climate agreement adopted in 2015.

It aims to strengthen the global response to climate change.

Important concepts include:

  • Limiting global temperature increase
  • Nationally determined contributions (NDCs)
  • Adaptation
  • Climate finance
  • Transparency

Important distinction

Kyoto Protocol → Earlier climate agreement with differentiated binding targets for developed countries under its framework

Paris Agreement → Broader participation through nationally determined contributions

49. International Solar Alliance (ISA)

The International Solar Alliance is an international organization focused on promoting solar energy cooperation.

It aims to encourage:

  • Solar energy deployment
  • International cooperation
  • Investment
  • Technology
  • Capacity building

Important exam point

The International Solar Alliance was launched as a collaborative initiative involving India and France.

Easy memory

ISA → Solar energy cooperation

52. Important Comparisons

MDGs vs SDGs

MDGs SDGs
8 goals 17 goals
Earlier framework Later framework
Strong focus on development challenges Integrated economic, social and environmental development

Global Warming vs Climate Change

Global Warming Climate Change
Long-term increase in average temperature Broader long-term changes in climate
One component of climate change Includes warming and other changes
Mainly refers to temperature increase Includes rainfall, temperature, sea level, extreme events, etc.

Hazard vs Disaster

Hazard Disaster
Potential source of harm Serious harmful event/disruption
May or may not cause damage Causes significant impacts
Example: cyclone approaching an area Severe cyclone causing widespread damage

Renewable vs Non-Renewable Resources

Renewable Non-Renewable
Can replenish naturally Limited stock
Solar Coal
Wind Petroleum
Hydro Natural gas
Biomass Many mineral resources

54. Important Environmental Terms

Biodiversity

The variety of living organisms and ecosystems.

Ecosystem

A system consisting of living organisms interacting with each other and with their physical environment.

Deforestation

Large-scale removal of forests.

Afforestation

Establishment of forests on land that was not recently forested.

Reforestation

Re-establishment of forest cover on previously forested land.

Desertification

Land degradation in dryland areas.

Pollution

Introduction of harmful substances or energy into the environment.

Conservation

Protection and careful management of natural resources.

Sustainability

Using resources in a way that supports present and future generations.

55. Important Cause-and-Effect Relationships

Deforestation

Deforestation → Loss of vegetation → Habitat loss → Biodiversity decline

Fossil fuel combustion

Fossil fuels → Greenhouse gas emissions → Enhanced greenhouse effect → Global warming → Climate change

Excess nutrients in water

Fertilizers/sewage → Nutrient enrichment → Algal bloom → Oxygen depletion → Aquatic damage

Improper waste disposal

Waste → Soil/water contamination → Pollutant exposure → Health/environmental impacts

57. Climate Change: Mitigation vs Adaptation

Mitigation

Mitigation means reducing the causes of climate change.

Examples:

  • Renewable energy
  • Energy efficiency
  • Public transportation
  • Afforestation
  • Reduced greenhouse gas emissions

Adaptation

Adaptation means adjusting to actual or expected climate impacts.

Examples:

  • Drought-resistant crops
  • Flood-resistant infrastructure
  • Heat action plans
  • Water conservation
  • Coastal protection

Easy memory

Mitigation → Reduce the cause

Adaptation → Adjust to the impact

58. Environmental Management

Environmental management means planning and implementing actions to protect environmental quality and natural resources.

Important approaches include:

  • Pollution prevention
  • Resource conservation
  • Waste management
  • Environmental monitoring
  • Sustainable production
  • Environmental awareness
  • Disaster preparedness

59. Role of Individuals in Environmental Protection

Individuals can contribute through:

  • Saving electricity
  • Saving water
  • Reducing unnecessary consumption
  • Using public transportation
  • Avoiding single-use plastics
  • Segregating waste
  • Recycling
  • Planting and protecting trees
  • Responsible e-waste disposal

Small actions become significant when practiced by large populations.
