Unit III – Comprehension

1. Introduction

The UGC NET Paper 1 syllabus for Unit III states:

A passage of text be given. Questions be asked from the passage to be answered.

Therefore, this unit primarily tests the ability of a candidate to read, understand, interpret and answer questions based on a given passage.

Comprehension is not simply reading the passage. It involves understanding:

  • The main idea
  • Important details
  • Meaning of statements
  • Relationships between ideas
  • Author’s purpose
  • Conclusions that can be drawn from the passage
  • Information explicitly stated in the passage

2. What is Comprehension?

Comprehension is the ability to understand and interpret written information.

When we read a passage, we should be able to answer:

  1. What is the passage about?
  2. What is the main idea?
  3. What are the important points?
  4. What does the author mean?
  5. What conclusion can be drawn?
  6. Which statements are supported by the passage?

Simple Example

Consider:

Online education provides learners with access to educational resources from different locations. It can also provide flexibility for learners who cannot attend traditional classrooms. However, successful online learning requires appropriate technology, self-discipline and active participation.

The central idea is:

Online education provides flexibility and access, but successful learning requires certain conditions.

3. Purpose of Comprehension Questions

Comprehension questions may test different reading abilities.

Important abilities include:

  • Identifying information
  • Understanding meaning
  • Finding the main idea
  • Identifying supporting details
  • Understanding relationships
  • Interpreting statements
  • Drawing conclusions
  • Identifying the author’s purpose

4. How to Read a Passage

A good approach is to read the passage actively rather than reading every sentence mechanically.

Step 1 – Read the Passage Carefully

Read the complete passage once.

Try to understand the overall topic.

Do not immediately try to memorize every sentence.

Step 2 – Identify the Topic

Ask:

“What is this passage mainly about?”

The topic may be:

  • Education
  • Technology
  • Environment
  • Science
  • Society
  • Research
  • Communication
  • Development

The topic is usually broader than the main argument.

Step 3 – Identify the Main Idea

The main idea is the central point the author wants to communicate.

Example

If a passage discusses:

  • Benefits of renewable energy
  • Reduction of pollution
  • Energy security
  • Sustainable development

the main idea may be:

Renewable energy can contribute to sustainable development and reduce dependence on conventional energy sources.

5. Topic vs Main Idea

These two concepts are important.

Topic Main Idea
General subject Central message
Usually short Usually a complete statement
Example: Online education Online education provides flexibility but requires effective learner participation

Easy Trick

Ask:

Topic = What is it about?

Main idea = What is the author saying about it?

6. Supporting Details

Supporting details are pieces of information that explain, demonstrate or support the main idea.

They may include:

  • Examples
  • Facts
  • Reasons
  • Explanations
  • Statistics
  • Comparisons
  • Arguments

Example

Main idea:

Online learning provides flexibility.

Supporting details may include:

  • Students can access materials remotely.
  • Recorded lectures can be viewed later.
  • Learners can study at different times.

7. Explicit Information

Explicit information is information that is directly stated in the passage.

Example

Passage:

“The university introduced online examinations in 2024.”

Question:

When did the university introduce online examinations?

Answer:

2024

The answer is directly available in the passage.

Important Rule

For comprehension questions, always prefer information supported directly by the passage.

8. Inference

An inference is a conclusion that can reasonably be drawn from information provided in the passage.

The answer may not be directly stated.

Example

Passage:

Many students use recorded lectures because they can watch them after completing their regular classes.

Possible inference:

Students value the flexibility provided by recorded lectures.

The exact statement may not appear in the passage, but it can reasonably be inferred.

Important Rule

An inference must be supported by the passage.

Do not use your personal opinion.

9. Main Idea Questions

A common type of comprehension question asks about the main idea.

Typical Questions

  • What is the passage mainly about?
  • What is the central idea of the passage?
  • What is the primary concern of the author?
  • Which statement best summarizes the passage?

How to Answer

  1. Read the entire passage.
  2. Identify the repeated or central theme.
  3. Examine the introduction and conclusion.
  4. Eliminate options that discuss only one small detail.
  5. Select the option that covers the passage as a whole.

10. Detail-Based Questions

These questions ask for information specifically mentioned in the passage.

Examples

  • According to the passage, what is…?
  • Which of the following is mentioned?
  • What reason does the author give?
  • According to the passage, which statement is true?

Strategy

Return to the relevant part of the passage and verify the answer.

Do not depend only on memory.

11. Vocabulary in Context

Sometimes the meaning of a word must be understood from its context.

Example

“The researcher adopted a novel approach to the problem.”

Here, novel means:

New or innovative

The word should be interpreted according to the sentence rather than automatically using a familiar meaning.

Strategy

Read:

  • The sentence containing the word.
  • The previous sentence.
  • The following sentence.

The surrounding context often gives clues.

12. Author’s Purpose

A question may ask why the author wrote the passage.

Possible purposes include:

  • To explain
  • To describe
  • To inform
  • To compare
  • To criticize
  • To argue
  • To persuade
  • To discuss a problem
  • To present information

Example

If a passage explains how a technology works, the author’s purpose may be:

To explain the technology.

13. Tone of the Passage

Tone refers to the attitude expressed by the author.

Possible tones include:

  • Positive
  • Negative
  • Neutral
  • Critical
  • Supportive
  • Concerned
  • Analytical
  • Informative
  • Optimistic
  • Cautious

Important

Do not identify the tone based on one word alone.

Consider the passage as a whole.

14. Fact and Opinion

Fact

A statement that can be verified.

Example:

India has a large higher education system.

Opinion

A statement expressing a belief, judgment or personal view.

Example:

Online education is the best form of education.

When answering comprehension questions, determine whether the passage presents something as a fact, opinion or argument.

15. Cause and Effect

A passage may explain relationships between causes and effects.

Cause

The reason something happens.

Effect

The result of something happening.

Example

Excessive use of fossil fuels increases greenhouse gas emissions, which contributes to climate change.

Excessive fossil fuel use
          ↓
Increased emissions
          ↓
Climate change

Questions may ask:

What is identified as a cause of the problem?

or:

What is the consequence described in the passage?

16. Comparison

Authors may compare two or more ideas.

Look for words such as:

  • Similarly
  • Likewise
  • Unlike
  • In contrast
  • However
  • Whereas
  • While
  • On the other hand

Example

Traditional classrooms require physical presence, whereas online learning can be accessed remotely.

The passage compares:

Traditional learning and online learning

17. Signal Words

Certain words help identify relationships between ideas.

Addition

  • Also
  • Furthermore
  • Moreover
  • In addition

Contrast

  • However
  • But
  • Although
  • Nevertheless
  • In contrast

Cause

  • Because
  • Since
  • Due to
  • As a result of

Effect

  • Therefore
  • Thus
  • Consequently
  • Hence

Example

Online education is flexible. However, it requires self-discipline.

The word however indicates contrast.

18. Understanding Pronoun References

Questions may ask what a word such as:

  • It
  • They
  • This
  • These
  • That
  • Such

refers to.

Example

Online education provides flexibility. This allows learners to study according to their schedules.

Here, This refers to:

The flexibility provided by online education.

Strategy

Look at the preceding sentence and identify the noun or idea that logically fits.

19. Understanding Statements

Sometimes a question asks:

Which of the following statements is correct according to the passage?

The candidate should compare each option with the passage.

Important

Do not select an option merely because it is generally true.

It must be supported by the passage.

20. True, False and Not Given

Some comprehension questions may effectively require distinguishing between:

True

The passage supports the statement.

False

The passage contradicts the statement.

Not Given

The passage does not provide enough information.

Example

Passage:

“Online courses provide learners with access to recorded lectures.”

Statement:

“All online courses provide recorded lectures.”

The statement is not necessarily supported because the passage says online courses provide access to recorded lectures but does not establish that all online courses do so.

21. Drawing Conclusions

A conclusion is a reasonable result that follows from the information presented.

Example

Passage:

Students who receive regular feedback are able to identify their mistakes and improve their performance.

Possible conclusion:

Regular feedback can support learning improvement.

The conclusion is based on the information given.

22. Avoiding Personal Opinion

This is one of the most important rules.

Suppose the passage discusses online education.

You may personally believe:

“Online education is better than classroom education.”

But if the passage does not say this, you should not select an answer based on your personal belief.

Rule

Answer from the passage, not from your personal knowledge or opinion.

23. Eliminating Wrong Options

When you are unsure, eliminate clearly incorrect options.

Common wrong options include:

23.1 Completely Unrelated Option

The option discusses something not mentioned in the passage.

23.2 Too Extreme

Words such as:

  • Always
  • Never
  • All
  • None
  • Completely
  • Only

can make an option too broad when the passage does not support such a claim.

23.3 Partially Correct Option

The option may contain information from the passage but does not answer the question completely.

23.4 Personal Opinion

The option may sound reasonable but is not supported by the passage.

24. A Practical Method for UGC NET

Use the following sequence:

Read Passage
     ↓
Identify Topic
     ↓
Identify Main Idea
     ↓
Notice Important Details
     ↓
Read Question
     ↓
Locate Relevant Information
     ↓
Compare Options
     ↓
Eliminate Wrong Options
     ↓
Select the Answer Supported by Passage

25. Time Management

Comprehension questions should be approached efficiently.

Recommended Strategy

  1. Read the passage carefully.
  2. Do not spend too much time memorizing every sentence.
  3. Read the questions carefully.
  4. Return to the relevant section when necessary.
  5. Eliminate clearly incorrect options.
  6. Select the answer supported by the passage.

Important

Do not spend excessive time on one difficult question.

26. Mini Practice Passage

Read the following passage:

Digital technology has changed the way students access educational resources. Online platforms allow learners to access lectures, books and other learning materials from different locations. However, access to technology alone does not guarantee effective learning. Students also need motivation, appropriate learning strategies and the ability to use digital resources effectively.

Question 1

What is the main idea of the passage?

A. Digital technology has completely replaced traditional education.

B. Digital technology provides educational access, but effective learning also depends on other factors.

C. Students should only use online learning platforms.

D. Traditional education is no longer useful.

Answer

B

Explanation

The passage explains both:

  • The benefits of digital technology.
  • The additional factors required for effective learning.

Therefore, option B represents the main idea.

Question 2

According to the passage, what can online platforms provide?

A. Only examinations

B. Only classroom teaching

C. Access to lectures, books and learning materials

D. Guaranteed academic success

Answer

C

The answer is directly stated in the passage.

Question 3

Which factor is NOT sufficient by itself to guarantee effective learning?

A. Access to technology

B. Motivation

C. Learning strategies

D. Ability to use digital resources

Answer

A

The passage specifically states that access to technology alone does not guarantee effective learning.

27. Common Mistakes

Mistake 1 – Reading Too Quickly

Reading quickly without understanding the structure can lead to incorrect answers.

Mistake 2 – Using Outside Knowledge

The question is generally based on the passage.

Mistake 3 – Choosing the Longest Option

A longer option is not automatically correct.

Mistake 4 – Ignoring Qualifying Words

Words such as:

  • Some
  • Many
  • Often
  • Usually
  • May
  • Can

can change the meaning of a statement.

Mistake 5 – Choosing an Extreme Answer

An answer containing “always” or “never” may not be supported by the passage.

Mistake 6 – Not Reading the Question Carefully

Words such as NOT, EXCEPT, incorrect, and least are important.

28. Important Words in Questions

Pay special attention to:

Word Meaning
Main Central
Primarily Mainly
According to Based on the passage
Infer Draw a conclusion
Implies Suggests indirectly
Except Find the different/incorrect option
NOT Find the option that does not apply
Purpose Reason for writing
Tone Author’s attitude
Summarize Give the main points briefly

29. Quick Revision

  1. Comprehension tests the ability to understand written information.
  2. The topic tells us what the passage is about.
  3. The main idea tells us what the author is saying about the topic.
  4. Supporting details explain or support the main idea.
  5. Explicit information is directly stated.
  6. An inference is a reasonable conclusion based on the passage.
  7. Vocabulary should be understood in context.
  8. Author’s purpose explains why the passage was written.
  9. Tone represents the author’s attitude.
  10. Cause explains why something happens.
  11. Effect explains the result.
  12. Signal words help identify relationships between ideas.
  13. Answers should be based on the passage.
  14. Personal opinions should not replace evidence from the passage.
  15. Elimination can help when options are confusing.
  16. Pay special attention to words such as NOT, EXCEPT, INCORRECT, MOST and LEAST.

30. UGC NET Exam Strategy

Remember these five rules:

Rule 1

Read for meaning, not memorization.

Rule 2

Identify the main idea before answering.

Rule 3

Use evidence from the passage.

Rule 4

Do not bring your personal opinion into the answer.

Rule 5

Read every option carefully before selecting the answer.

32. Final Exam Tip

The most important principle for comprehension questions is:

The passage is the evidence.

Even if you know something about the topic from your own knowledge, use that knowledge only to understand the passage. The answer should be supported by what the passage actually says or reasonably implies.
