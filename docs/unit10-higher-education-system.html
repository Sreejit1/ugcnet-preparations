Unit X – Higher Education System

1. Introduction

Higher education refers to education received after school-level education.

It includes:

  • Undergraduate education
  • Postgraduate education
  • Research programmes
  • Professional education
  • Technical education
  • Skill-based education
  • Doctoral education

Higher education plays an important role in:

  • Knowledge creation
  • Research
  • Innovation
  • Employment
  • Economic development
  • Social development
  • Nation building

India has a long tradition of higher learning, beginning with ancient centres of learning and continuing through universities, colleges, research institutions and professional institutions in modern India.

2. Institutions of Higher Learning in Ancient India

India has a long history of organized learning.

Ancient Indian education was provided through institutions and traditions such as:

  • Gurukulas
  • Ashramas
  • Buddhist monasteries
  • Jain centres of learning
  • Ancient centres of higher learning

Education was not limited to one subject.

Students could study:

  • Philosophy
  • Logic
  • Grammar
  • Mathematics
  • Medicine
  • Astronomy
  • Literature
  • Politics
  • Economics
  • Religion
  • Philosophy

3. Gurukula System

The Gurukula system was an ancient Indian system of education in which students lived with or near the teacher, known as the guru.

The relationship between teacher and student was important.

Main characteristics

  • Close teacher-student relationship
  • Residential learning
  • Discipline
  • Moral development
  • Practical learning
  • Oral tradition
  • Individual attention

The teacher guided students not only in academic matters but also in values and conduct.

4. Important Ancient Centres of Learning

Several ancient Indian centres became famous for higher learning.

Important examples include:

  • Takshashila
  • Nalanda
  • Vikramashila
  • Vallabhi
  • Odantapuri

5. Takshashila

Takshashila was an ancient centre of learning located in the northwestern part of the Indian subcontinent.

It became known for advanced learning in areas such as:

  • Medicine
  • Political science
  • Philosophy
  • Grammar
  • Military studies

Important point

Takshashila is generally regarded as one of the earliest important centres of higher learning in ancient India.

6. Nalanda

Nalanda was a major ancient centre of Buddhist learning in present-day Bihar.

It attracted students and scholars from different parts of Asia.

Subjects included:

  • Buddhist philosophy
  • Logic
  • Grammar
  • Medicine
  • Astronomy
  • Philosophy

Important features

  • Large residential learning centre
  • International scholars
  • Extensive scholarly traditions
  • Libraries
  • Debate and discussion

UGC NET Focus

Nalanda → Ancient centre of higher learning → Bihar

7. Vikramashila

Vikramashila was another important Buddhist centre of learning in ancient India.

It was established under the Pala dynasty.

It became particularly important as a centre for:

  • Buddhist philosophy
  • Logic
  • Scholarly studies

8. Vallabhi

Vallabhi was an important centre of learning in ancient India.

It was located in present-day Gujarat.

It became known for studies in areas including:

  • Religion
  • Philosophy
  • Administration
  • Other branches of knowledge

9. Odantapuri

Odantapuri was an important Buddhist centre of learning associated with the Pala period.

It was located in present-day Bihar.

It formed part of the network of Buddhist centres of higher learning in eastern India.

10. Features of Ancient Indian Higher Learning

Ancient Indian higher learning emphasized:

  • Teacher-student relationship
  • Debate
  • Discussion
  • Reasoning
  • Philosophy
  • Ethics
  • Character development
  • Knowledge transmission
  • Residential learning

Important idea

Ancient education was not concerned only with employment.

It also emphasized:

Knowledge + Values + Character + Social responsibility

11. Higher Education During the Colonial Period

The development of modern higher education institutions in India was strongly influenced by colonial administration.

Important developments included the establishment of modern universities and institutions.

Universities established in 1857

Three important universities were established in:

  • Calcutta
  • Bombay
  • Madras

These became important institutions in the development of modern higher education in India.

12. Post-Independence Higher Education

India became independent in 1947.

After independence, higher education became an important instrument for:

  • Nation building
  • Scientific development
  • Social development
  • Economic development
  • Research
  • Technological development

The country began establishing and expanding:

  • Universities
  • Colleges
  • IITs
  • IIMs
  • Research institutions
  • Medical institutions
  • Agricultural institutions
  • Technical institutions

13. University Education Commission, 1948–49

The University Education Commission was established after independence.

It was chaired by:

Dr. S. Radhakrishnan

Therefore, it is commonly called the:

Radhakrishnan Commission

Main areas of concern

  • University education
  • Teaching
  • Research
  • Teacher quality
  • University standards
  • Higher education development

UGC NET Point

University Education Commission → Radhakrishnan → 1948–49

14. University Grants Commission (UGC)

The University Grants Commission (UGC) is an important statutory body associated with higher education in India.

It was established as a statutory body under the UGC Act, 1956.

Major functions

UGC is associated with:

  • Coordination of university education
  • Maintaining standards
  • Promotion of teaching and research
  • Providing grants
  • Advising governments
  • Supporting higher education development

Easy memory

UGC → Coordination + Standards + Grants

15. Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs)

The Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs) are major institutions for:

  • Engineering
  • Technology
  • Scientific education
  • Research
  • Innovation

They have contributed significantly to India’s technical and scientific development.

16. Indian Institutes of Management (IIMs)

The Indian Institutes of Management (IIMs) are major institutions for management education and research.

They focus on:

  • Management
  • Business
  • Leadership
  • Research
  • Entrepreneurship

17. Evolution of Research in Post-Independence India

Research became an important part of India’s nation-building strategy.

Major areas include:

  • Science
  • Technology
  • Agriculture
  • Medicine
  • Engineering
  • Social sciences
  • Humanities
  • Environmental studies
  • Space research

Research institutions and universities became important centres of knowledge production.

18. Indian Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR)

CSIR is a major Indian organization associated with scientific and industrial research.

Its research areas include:

  • Science
  • Engineering
  • Industry
  • Healthcare
  • Environment
  • Technology

It operates through a network of research laboratories.

19. Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO)

ISRO is India’s major space research and space technology organization.

Its work includes:

  • Space science
  • Satellites
  • Launch vehicles
  • Remote sensing
  • Communication
  • Navigation
  • Space applications

Research organizations such as ISRO have contributed to India’s technological development.

20. Professional Education

Professional education prepares students for specific professions.

Examples:

  • Medicine
  • Law
  • Engineering
  • Management
  • Architecture
  • Pharmacy
  • Nursing
  • Teaching

Main characteristic

Professional education combines:

Knowledge + Practical training + Professional skills

21. Technical Education

Technical education focuses on scientific, technological and engineering knowledge and skills.

Examples:

  • Engineering
  • Information technology
  • Computer science
  • Electronics
  • Mechanical engineering
  • Civil engineering
  • Polytechnic education

Objectives

  • Develop technical skills
  • Support industries
  • Promote innovation
  • Prepare skilled professionals

22. Skill-Based Education

Skill-based education focuses on developing practical abilities that can be applied in real-world situations.

Examples:

  • Programming
  • Data analysis
  • Electrical work
  • Welding
  • Automobile repair
  • Digital marketing
  • Graphic design
  • Healthcare support
  • Entrepreneurship

Difference from traditional education

Traditional education may emphasize:

Knowledge and theory

Skill-based education emphasizes:

Knowledge + Application + Practical ability

23. Vocational Education

Vocational education prepares learners for particular occupations or trades.

Examples:

  • Automotive services
  • Electrical work
  • Plumbing
  • Hospitality
  • Healthcare support
  • Information technology

Vocational education can improve:

  • Employability
  • Practical skills
  • Entrepreneurship
  • Workforce readiness

24. Oriental Learning Programmes

Oriental learning generally refers to traditional learning programmes associated with classical Indian and Asian knowledge traditions.

In the Indian context, it may include studies related to:

  • Sanskrit
  • Classical languages
  • Indian philosophy
  • Traditional literature
  • Ancient texts
  • Classical knowledge systems

Examples include studies of:

  • Sanskrit
  • Pali
  • Prakrit
  • Classical Indian philosophy

25. Conventional Learning Programmes

Conventional learning refers to traditional formal education programmes.

Examples:

  • Regular classroom programmes
  • Undergraduate programmes
  • Postgraduate programmes
  • Conventional university courses

Characteristics

  • Structured curriculum
  • Teacher-led instruction
  • Fixed academic schedule
  • Physical classrooms in many cases
  • Formal examinations

26. Non-Conventional Learning Programmes

Non-conventional learning provides alternatives to the traditional classroom system.

Examples:

  • Distance education
  • Online education
  • Open learning
  • MOOCs
  • Digital learning
  • Correspondence education

Advantages

  • Flexible
  • Accessible
  • Suitable for working learners
  • Can reach geographically distant students

27. Open and Distance Learning

Open and Distance Learning (ODL) provides education without requiring students to attend conventional classrooms regularly.

It may use:

  • Printed materials
  • Online materials
  • Video lectures
  • Learning management systems
  • Contact classes
  • Digital resources

Important Indian institution

Indira Gandhi National Open University (IGNOU) is a major institution associated with open and distance learning.

28. MOOCs

MOOC means:

Massive Open Online Course

MOOCs allow large numbers of learners to access courses online.

Characteristics include:

  • Online learning
  • Large-scale participation
  • Open access to learning resources
  • Video lectures
  • Assessments
  • Discussion forums

Indian example

SWAYAM provides online courses and is an important initiative in India’s digital education ecosystem.

29. Professional vs Technical vs Skill-Based Education

Professional Technical Skill-Based
Prepares for professions Focuses on technology Focuses on practical abilities
Medicine, law Engineering, IT Programming, electrical work
Professional practice Technical knowledge Job-related skills
Often requires professional qualification Technical qualification Competency-focused

30. Value Education

Value education aims to develop desirable values, attitudes and behaviour.

Important values include:

  • Honesty
  • Responsibility
  • Respect
  • Empathy
  • Cooperation
  • Justice
  • Equality
  • Integrity
  • Tolerance
  • Social responsibility

31. Objectives of Value Education

Value education helps students:

  • Develop ethical behaviour
  • Understand social responsibility
  • Respect others
  • Develop integrity
  • Make responsible decisions
  • Develop empathy
  • Become responsible citizens

Important idea

Education should develop not only:

Knowledge

but also:

Character + Values + Responsibility

32. Environmental Education

Environmental education develops knowledge, awareness, attitudes, skills and participation related to the environment.

It helps learners understand:

  • Environmental problems
  • Natural resources
  • Pollution
  • Biodiversity
  • Climate change
  • Conservation
  • Sustainable development

33. Objectives of Environmental Education

Environmental education aims to develop:

Awareness

Understanding environmental issues.

Knowledge

Understanding ecosystems and environmental problems.

Attitude

Developing concern for the environment.

Skills

Developing the ability to identify and solve environmental problems.

Participation

Encouraging people to participate in environmental protection.

34. Value Education vs Environmental Education

Value Education Environmental Education
Focuses on values and ethics Focuses on environment
Honesty, empathy, responsibility Pollution, biodiversity, conservation
Develops ethical behaviour Develops environmental awareness
Social responsibility Environmental responsibility

35. Policies in Higher Education

Higher education policies determine how higher education is:

  • Planned
  • Funded
  • Regulated
  • Administered
  • Expanded
  • Evaluated

Important policy areas include:

  • Access
  • Equity
  • Quality
  • Research
  • Innovation
  • Skill development
  • Digital education
  • Internationalization
  • Institutional autonomy

36. National Education Policy (NEP) 2020

The National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 is an important education policy framework in India.

It covers both school and higher education.

Important ideas in higher education include:

  • Multidisciplinary education
  • Flexible learning
  • Multiple entry and exit
  • Academic Bank of Credits
  • Research promotion
  • Institutional autonomy
  • Technology integration
  • Skill development
  • Internationalization
  • Greater access and inclusion

37. Multidisciplinary Education

Multidisciplinary education allows students to study subjects from different academic disciplines.

For example, a student may combine:

  • Computer science
  • Economics
  • Mathematics
  • Psychology

This approach encourages:

  • Broader knowledge
  • Interdisciplinary thinking
  • Problem-solving
  • Innovation

38. Multiple Entry and Exit

One of the important ideas associated with NEP 2020 is greater flexibility in undergraduate education.

Depending on the programme and applicable regulations, students may receive qualifications at different stages.

The broad concept is:

Flexible entry + Flexible exit + Re-entry opportunities

This can help learners continue education according to their circumstances.

39. Academic Bank of Credits

The Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) is designed to facilitate the digital storage and transfer of academic credits.

Basic idea

Student earns credits

↓

Credits are digitally recorded

↓

Eligible credits can be transferred/accumulated according to applicable rules

↓

Supports flexible learning pathways

Important point

ABC → Academic credits

40. Governance in Higher Education

Governance refers to the processes through which institutions are:

  • Directed
  • Managed
  • Regulated
  • Accountable

Higher education governance involves:

  • Government
  • Regulatory bodies
  • Universities
  • Colleges
  • Academic councils
  • Administrators
  • Faculty
  • Students
  • Other stakeholders

41. Administration in Higher Education

Administration refers to the day-to-day management and implementation of institutional activities.

It includes:

  • Admissions
  • Examinations
  • Curriculum implementation
  • Finance
  • Human resources
  • Student services
  • Infrastructure
  • Records
  • Academic scheduling

42. Governance vs Administration

Governance Administration
Direction and oversight Day-to-day implementation
Policy and strategic decisions Operational activities
Accountability Execution
Long-term institutional direction Routine management

Simple example

Governance:

“University should improve research quality.”

Administration:

“Create research guidelines, allocate funds and organize research activities.”

43. Regulatory Bodies and Institutions

Higher education in India involves different organizations and regulatory structures.

Important names include:

  • UGC
  • AICTE
  • NCTE
  • NMC
  • NAAC
  • NIRF
  • Universities
  • Higher education departments

Different organizations have different roles and mandates.

44. UGC

University Grants Commission

Important functions include:

  • Coordination
  • Standards
  • Grants
  • Promotion of higher education
  • Research and academic development

45. AICTE

AICTE = All India Council for Technical Education

It is associated with the development and coordinated growth of technical education.

Areas include:

  • Engineering
  • Technology
  • Management
  • Pharmacy and other technical education areas under its mandate

Exam memory

AICTE → Technical Education

46. NCTE

NCTE = National Council for Teacher Education

It is associated with teacher education.

Exam memory

NCTE → Teacher Education

47. NAAC

NAAC = National Assessment and Accreditation Council

It is associated with assessment and accreditation of higher education institutions.

Basic purpose

To assess and support quality-related processes in higher education institutions.

Exam memory

NAAC → Assessment + Accreditation

48. NIRF

NIRF = National Institutional Ranking Framework

It provides a framework for ranking higher education institutions in India.

It considers various parameters related to institutional performance.

Exam memory

NIRF → Ranking

49. Quality in Higher Education

Quality in higher education can involve:

  • Teaching quality
  • Learning outcomes
  • Research
  • Infrastructure
  • Faculty
  • Student support
  • Governance
  • Innovation
  • Institutional performance

Quality assurance helps institutions continuously improve.

50. Autonomy in Higher Education

Institutional autonomy means giving institutions an appropriate degree of freedom to make academic, administrative or other decisions within the applicable regulatory framework.

Examples may include:

  • Curriculum development
  • Academic programmes
  • Research activities
  • Institutional planning

Important balance

Autonomy + Accountability

Autonomy does not mean absence of regulation or responsibility.

51. Equity and Inclusion

Higher education should provide opportunities to learners from different backgrounds.

Important areas include:

  • Gender equality
  • Socio-economic inclusion
  • Disability inclusion
  • Regional access
  • Support for disadvantaged groups

Important principle

Access + Equity + Quality

are important goals of higher education.

52. Digital Transformation in Higher Education

Technology has changed higher education through:

  • Online classes
  • Learning Management Systems
  • Digital libraries
  • MOOCs
  • Online examinations
  • Video conferencing
  • Digital academic records
  • Virtual laboratories
  • Online research databases

Digital education can improve access but also creates challenges such as:

  • Digital divide
  • Connectivity problems
  • Device affordability
  • Digital literacy
  • Cybersecurity

53. Research and Innovation in Higher Education

Universities play an important role in:

  • Knowledge creation
  • Scientific research
  • Innovation
  • Patents
  • Entrepreneurship
  • Technology transfer

Research contributes to:

  • Economic development
  • Social development
  • Scientific advancement
  • Problem solving

54. Higher Education and Skill Development

Modern higher education increasingly connects academic learning with employability.

Important areas include:

  • Internships
  • Industry interaction
  • Practical projects
  • Entrepreneurship
  • Vocational education
  • Skill development
  • Experiential learning

Important idea

Education + Skills + Experience → Better preparation for employment

56. Higher Education and Nation Building

Higher education contributes to nation building through:

  • Skilled human resources
  • Research
  • Innovation
  • Entrepreneurship
  • Social development
  • Scientific advancement
  • Democratic participation
  • Cultural development

A strong higher education system therefore contributes to both individual and national development.
