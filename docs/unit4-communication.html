Unit IV – Communication

1. Introduction

Communication is an essential part of human life. It is used to:

  • Share information
  • Express ideas
  • Exchange opinions
  • Convey feelings
  • Give instructions
  • Build relationships
  • Teach and learn
  • Coordinate activities

In education, effective communication between teachers and learners is essential for successful teaching and learning.

2. Meaning of Communication

Communication is the process of sharing information, ideas, thoughts, feelings or messages between individuals or groups.

The word communication is derived from the Latin word “communicare”, which is associated with sharing or making something common.

Simple Definition

Communication is the process through which a sender conveys a message to a receiver and the receiver interprets the message.

Basic Communication Process

Sender
   ↓
Message
   ↓
Encoding
   ↓
Channel
   ↓
Receiver
   ↓
Decoding
   ↓
Feedback

Noise or interference can affect any stage of communication.

3. Elements of Communication

The major elements of communication are:

  1. Sender
  2. Encoding
  3. Message
  4. Channel
  5. Receiver
  6. Decoding
  7. Feedback
  8. Noise
  9. Context

3.1 Sender

The sender is the person or organization that initiates the communication.

Example

A teacher explaining a lesson is the sender.

3.2 Encoding

Encoding is the process of converting an idea or thought into a form that can be communicated.

This may involve:

  • Words
  • Symbols
  • Images
  • Gestures
  • Sounds

Example

A teacher converts an idea into spoken words while explaining a topic.

3.3 Message

The message is the information, idea, thought or feeling being communicated.

Example

“The examination will be conducted next Monday.”

This information is the message.

3.4 Channel

The channel is the medium through which the message is transmitted.

Examples:

  • Face-to-face conversation
  • Telephone
  • Email
  • Letter
  • Television
  • Radio
  • Internet
  • Video conference

3.5 Receiver

The receiver is the person or group for whom the message is intended.

Example

In classroom teaching:

Teacher → Sender

Students → Receivers

3.6 Decoding

Decoding is the process by which the receiver interprets the message.

For example, a student listens to the teacher and understands the explanation.

3.7 Feedback

Feedback is the response given by the receiver to the sender.

Examples:

  • Answering a question
  • Asking a question
  • Nodding
  • Sending a reply
  • Completing an assignment

Feedback helps the sender determine whether the message has been understood.

3.8 Noise

Noise refers to anything that interferes with communication.

Examples:

  • Loud sounds
  • Poor internet connection
  • Unclear language
  • Technical problems
  • Lack of attention
  • Psychological distractions

3.9 Context

Context refers to the situation or circumstances in which communication takes place.

The same message may have different meanings depending on the context.

4. Characteristics of Communication

Important characteristics of communication include:

4.1 Communication is a Process

Communication involves a sequence of activities rather than a single event.

4.2 Communication is Two-Way

Effective communication generally involves exchange between sender and receiver.

4.3 Communication is Continuous

People communicate continuously through speech, writing, gestures and other forms.

4.4 Communication is Dynamic

Communication changes according to:

  • Situation
  • People
  • Technology
  • Culture
  • Context

4.5 Communication Requires a Message

There must be some information, idea, feeling or thought to communicate.

4.6 Communication Requires a Medium

The message must be transmitted through an appropriate channel.

4.7 Communication Requires Understanding

Simply sending a message does not guarantee effective communication. The receiver should understand the intended meaning.

4.8 Communication Can Be Verbal and Non-Verbal

Communication can occur through words as well as body language, gestures and other non-verbal signals.

5. Types of Communication

Communication can be classified in several ways.

Important types include:

  • Verbal communication
  • Non-verbal communication
  • Formal communication
  • Informal communication
  • Interpersonal communication
  • Group communication
  • Mass communication
  • Inter-cultural communication

6. Verbal Communication

Verbal communication uses words to convey a message.

It can be:

  • Oral
  • Written

6.1 Oral Communication

Oral communication uses spoken language.

Examples:

  • Conversation
  • Lecture
  • Interview
  • Telephone conversation
  • Speech
  • Classroom teaching
  • Video conference

Advantages

  • Immediate communication
  • Quick feedback
  • Opportunity for clarification
  • Useful for discussions

Limitations

  • May not provide a permanent record.
  • Meaning can sometimes be misunderstood.
  • Depends on language and pronunciation.

7. Written Communication

Written communication uses written words.

Examples:

  • Letters
  • Reports
  • Emails
  • Notices
  • Books
  • Research papers
  • Messages

Advantages

  • Provides a record.
  • Can be carefully prepared.
  • Useful for formal communication.
  • Can reach people who are not physically present.

Limitations

  • Feedback may be delayed.
  • Writing can sometimes be misunderstood.
  • Requires appropriate language and writing skills.

8. Non-Verbal Communication

Non-verbal communication involves conveying information without relying primarily on spoken or written words.

Examples include:

  • Facial expressions
  • Gestures
  • Posture
  • Eye contact
  • Body movements
  • Appearance
  • Physical distance
  • Tone and other vocal characteristics

Example

A teacher may smile and nod when a student gives a correct answer.

The teacher is communicating encouragement without necessarily using words.

9. Important Forms of Non-Verbal Communication

9.1 Facial Expressions

Facial expressions can communicate emotions such as:

  • Happiness
  • Sadness
  • Surprise
  • Anger
  • Confusion

9.2 Eye Contact

Eye contact can communicate:

  • Attention
  • Confidence
  • Interest
  • Engagement

In classroom communication, appropriate eye contact can help maintain student attention.

9.3 Gestures

Gestures are movements of the hands, arms or body used to communicate meaning.

Examples:

  • Pointing
  • Waving
  • Nodding
  • Raising a hand

9.4 Posture

Posture refers to the way a person positions the body.

Posture can communicate:

  • Confidence
  • Attention
  • Interest
  • Nervousness

9.5 Proxemics

Proxemics refers to the use of physical space and distance in communication.

The distance between people can vary according to:

  • Relationship
  • Culture
  • Situation
  • Social context

10. Verbal vs Non-Verbal Communication

Verbal Communication Non-Verbal Communication
Uses words Uses gestures, expressions and body language
Oral or written Facial expressions, posture, gestures, etc.
Can communicate detailed information Often communicates attitudes and emotions
Language is important Body language and context are important
Example: lecture Example: nodding

11. Effective Communication

Effective communication occurs when the intended message is clearly understood by the receiver.

Effective communication should be:

  • Clear
  • Correct
  • Complete
  • Concise
  • Relevant
  • Appropriate
  • Timely
  • Understandable

12. Principles of Effective Communication

A useful framework is the 7 Cs of communication.

12.1 Clarity

The message should be easy to understand.

Poor

Submit it soon.

Better

Submit the assignment by Friday at 5 PM.

12.2 Conciseness

The message should avoid unnecessary information.

12.3 Completeness

The message should contain the information required by the receiver.

12.4 Correctness

The information and language should be accurate.

12.5 Concreteness

The message should be specific rather than vague.

12.6 Courtesy

Communication should be respectful and appropriate.

12.7 Consideration

The sender should consider the receiver’s needs, knowledge and circumstances.

13. Inter-Cultural Communication

Inter-cultural communication refers to communication between people belonging to different cultural backgrounds.

Culture may influence:

  • Language
  • Values
  • Beliefs
  • Behaviour
  • Gestures
  • Communication styles
  • Social expectations

Example

A gesture that is considered acceptable in one culture may have a different meaning in another culture.

Therefore, effective inter-cultural communication requires:

  • Cultural awareness
  • Respect
  • Sensitivity
  • Appropriate language
  • Avoidance of stereotypes

14. Challenges in Inter-Cultural Communication

Common challenges include:

  • Language differences
  • Different cultural values
  • Different communication styles
  • Different meanings of gestures
  • Stereotypes
  • Ethnocentrism
  • Different social norms

15. Group Communication

Group communication occurs when communication takes place among members of a group.

Examples:

  • Classroom discussion
  • Team meeting
  • Seminar
  • Committee meeting
  • Group project

Characteristics

  • More than two participants
  • Exchange of ideas
  • Interaction among members
  • Feedback
  • Shared objective

16. Effective Group Communication

Effective group communication requires:

  • Active listening
  • Respect for others
  • Clear expression
  • Participation
  • Cooperation
  • Appropriate leadership
  • Constructive feedback

Problems in Group Communication

  • Dominance by one person
  • Lack of participation
  • Conflict
  • Misunderstanding
  • Poor listening
  • Group pressure

17. Classroom Communication

Classroom communication is communication between teachers and learners within an educational environment.

It may include:

  • Teacher explanation
  • Student questions
  • Discussions
  • Instructions
  • Feedback
  • Presentations
  • Non-verbal communication

18. Characteristics of Effective Classroom Communication

Effective classroom communication should be:

Clear

Students should understand what the teacher is explaining.

Interactive

Students should have opportunities to participate.

Appropriate

Language should be suitable for the learner’s level.

Inclusive

Different learners should be given opportunities to participate.

Feedback-Oriented

Teachers should check whether students understand the content.

19. Teacher’s Role in Classroom Communication

The teacher should:

  • Explain concepts clearly.
  • Encourage questions.
  • Listen to students.
  • Use appropriate examples.
  • Provide feedback.
  • Observe student reactions.
  • Use suitable teaching aids.
  • Encourage participation.

20. Student’s Role in Classroom Communication

Students should:

  • Listen actively.
  • Ask questions.
  • Participate in discussions.
  • Give feedback.
  • Clarify doubts.
  • Respect other participants.
  • Take notes when appropriate.

21. Barriers to Effective Communication

A communication barrier is anything that prevents or reduces effective transmission and understanding of a message.

Major barriers include:

  1. Physical barriers
  2. Semantic barriers
  3. Psychological barriers
  4. Cultural barriers
  5. Organizational barriers
  6. Technological barriers

22. Physical Barriers

Physical barriers are environmental factors that interfere with communication.

Examples:

  • Noise
  • Poor lighting
  • Distance
  • Poor seating arrangement
  • Temperature
  • Construction sounds

Classroom Example

A noisy classroom can make it difficult for students to hear the teacher.

23. Semantic Barriers

Semantic barriers arise because of differences or confusion in the meaning of words and language.

Examples:

  • Difficult terminology
  • Ambiguous words
  • Poor translation
  • Different meanings of words
  • Technical jargon

Example

A teacher uses highly technical terminology with beginners without explanation.

The students may not understand the message.

24. Psychological Barriers

Psychological barriers are related to the mental or emotional state of participants.

Examples:

  • Fear
  • Anxiety
  • Stress
  • Lack of confidence
  • Prejudice
  • Lack of attention
  • Anger

Classroom Example

A student may know the answer but avoid speaking because of fear of making a mistake.

25. Cultural Barriers

Cultural differences can affect communication.

Examples:

  • Different values
  • Different beliefs
  • Different gestures
  • Language differences
  • Social norms

26. Organizational Barriers

Organizational barriers may arise in institutions because of:

  • Complex hierarchy
  • Poor communication channels
  • Lack of coordination
  • Excessive bureaucracy
  • Information overload

27. Technological Barriers

Technology can also create communication problems.

Examples:

  • Poor internet connection
  • Hardware failure
  • Software problems
  • Audio problems
  • Video problems
  • Lack of digital skills

Online Classroom Example

If the teacher’s microphone fails, students may not receive the message clearly.

28. How to Overcome Communication Barriers

Communication barriers can be reduced through:

  • Using simple language
  • Providing clear information
  • Active listening
  • Asking questions
  • Providing feedback
  • Reducing environmental noise
  • Respecting cultural differences
  • Using appropriate communication channels
  • Checking understanding
  • Improving technological infrastructure

29. Mass Communication

Mass communication is communication in which information is transmitted to a large and dispersed audience, generally through mass media.

Examples:

  • Television
  • Radio
  • Newspapers
  • Magazines
  • Cinema
  • Internet-based media

30. Mass Media

Mass media are channels used to communicate information to large audiences.

Major forms include:

Print Media

  • Newspapers
  • Magazines
  • Books

Electronic Media

  • Radio
  • Television

Digital Media

  • Websites
  • Social media
  • Online news platforms
  • Digital video platforms

31. Characteristics of Mass Communication

Mass communication generally involves:

  • Large audience
  • Wide geographical reach
  • Use of communication technologies
  • Organized content production
  • Rapid distribution of information

32. Functions of Mass Media

Mass media can perform several functions.

32.1 Information

Mass media provide information about:

  • Current events
  • Science
  • Education
  • Society
  • Government activities
  • International events

32.2 Education

Mass media can support education through:

  • Educational programmes
  • Documentaries
  • Online courses
  • Educational videos
  • Public awareness programmes

32.3 Entertainment

Examples include:

  • Films
  • Music
  • Television programmes
  • Digital entertainment

32.4 Public Awareness

Mass media can spread awareness about:

  • Health
  • Environment
  • Education
  • Social issues
  • Public safety

33. Mass Media and Society

Mass media have an important relationship with society.

They can influence:

  • Public awareness
  • Social attitudes
  • Culture
  • Education
  • Political and social discussions
  • Consumer behaviour

However, the impact of media depends on factors such as the content, audience, medium and social context.

34. Positive Effects of Mass Media

Mass media can:

  • Spread information quickly.
  • Support education.
  • Increase public awareness.
  • Connect people across geographical boundaries.
  • Provide entertainment.
  • Support social campaigns.
  • Facilitate communication during emergencies.

35. Challenges Associated with Mass Media

Mass media can also create challenges such as:

  • Misinformation
  • Information overload
  • Biased presentation
  • Privacy concerns
  • Stereotyping
  • Manipulation of information
  • Spread of misleading content

Therefore, media literacy is important.

36. Media Literacy

Media literacy is the ability to access, understand, evaluate and critically analyze media messages.

A media-literate person should ask:

  1. Who created this message?
  2. Why was it created?
  3. What evidence is provided?
  4. Is the information reliable?
  5. Is the message presenting facts or opinions?
  6. Are important details missing?
  7. Could the information be misleading?

37. Communication Flow

Communication can flow in different directions within an organization.

Downward Communication

Information moves from higher levels to lower levels.

Management
    ↓
Teachers
    ↓
Students

Upward Communication

Information moves from lower levels to higher levels.

Students
    ↓
Teachers
    ↓
Management

Horizontal Communication

Communication takes place between people at the same organizational level.

Teacher ↔ Teacher

41. UGC NET Important Points

Remember these points:

  1. Communication involves sharing information, ideas or feelings.
  2. The sender initiates the communication.
  3. Encoding converts an idea into a communicable form.
  4. Decoding is interpretation of the message by the receiver.
  5. Feedback is the receiver’s response.
  6. Noise interferes with communication.
  7. Verbal communication uses words.
  8. Non-verbal communication includes gestures, facial expressions and posture.
  9. Inter-cultural communication occurs between people from different cultural backgrounds.
  10. Group communication involves communication among members of a group.
  11. Classroom communication is central to the teaching-learning process.
  12. Physical, semantic, psychological, cultural, organizational and technological factors can create barriers.
  13. Mass communication reaches a large and dispersed audience.
  14. Newspapers, radio, television and digital media are forms of mass media.
  15. Mass media can inform, educate and entertain.
  16. Media literacy helps individuals critically evaluate media messages.

43. Exam Strategy

For UGC NET questions on communication, focus particularly on:

  • Elements of communication
  • Characteristics of communication
  • Verbal and non-verbal communication
  • Oral and written communication
  • Effective communication
  • Inter-cultural communication
  • Group communication
  • Classroom communication
  • Communication barriers
  • Mass communication
  • Mass media
  • Functions of mass media
  • Media literacy

When solving questions, carefully distinguish between:

Sender → Encoding → Message → Channel → Receiver → Decoding → Feedback

This sequence is particularly useful for questions about the communication process.
